The klaxon for second shift went at 0600, as it did every day on Halden Deep, and she was already dressed. Teodora Kask had been Nine for three years, since the morning they had issued her bunk nine in the cadet dormitory, and nobody but the station’s records called her anything else.

She was nineteen, narrow and quick, with a shaved head and a burn scar across the back of her right hand. Her eyes were the brown of strong tea. She ate standing up, always, and she never wasted a ration chit.

Halden Deep was a research station sunk two hundred metres under the Vell ice shelf. It ran on geothermal heat and recycled air, and it held sixty-two people. Its corridors were painted the same pale green from end to end, and the hum of the pumps never stopped.

Ada was in the galley when Nine came through, hunched over a mug with both hands. Ada was Fourteen, a year below her, a big freckled girl from the farm domes with green eyes and a laugh you could hear two decks away. Ada shared the bunk under hers, and they had been friends since their first week.

“Okafor wants you,” Ada said. “Dock Three. She said now, not in five minutes.”

Commander Okafor ran Halden Deep. She was a tall grey-haired woman of sixty who had served on the ice for thirty years, and she walked with a stick since the accident at Dock Three. The cadets called her the Old Woman, though never where she could hear.

Dock Three was the station’s only way out. It was a cold steel hangar at the bottom of the Spine, the long vertical shaft that joined all nine decks, and it smelt of oil and seawater. Two submersibles hung in cradles over the moon pool.

Voss was waiting with the commander. Marek Voss was the chief engineer, a heavy man with a black beard and oil in every crease of his knuckles, and he had built half the pumps on the station with his own hands. He nodded to Nine and said nothing.

“Cadet Kask,” said Okafor. “You will take the Petrel down to the north vent with Mr Voss. The rule is the same as ever: no one leaves the station alone, and no one stays out past four hours.”

Nine had never been outside. She said yes, Commander, before her voice could shake.
