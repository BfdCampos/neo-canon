The flood came in the second week of April. By noon the Sare was over the meadows and into the mill yard, brown and fast, carrying fence posts.

Dr Haddon drove his blue Rover down Bridge Street as far as the water would let him, then left it and walked. His half-moon spectacles were spotted with rain.

The Corn Exchange was opened for the families from the low streets. They slept in the Long Gallery under the aldermen, and someone lit a fire in the grate. Constance Haddon ran it all from a trestle table, tall and fair and furious.

The red-brick Corn Exchange stood on the high side of the square, and the water never reached it.

In Brennoch that week, the Reverend Lyle, who was supposed to be dying, carried sandbags from first light until dark.
