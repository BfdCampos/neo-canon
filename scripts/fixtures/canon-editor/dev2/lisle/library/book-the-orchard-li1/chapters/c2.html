Tamsin got off the train at Lisle Halt with one bag and a cold. The platform was empty except for her brother and the grey mare, and Ivo looked older than he had at Easter.

She was the clever one, their mother used to say; Ivo was the good one. Tamsin had their mother’s dark hair and their father’s temper, and at thirty she was still not married, which the village found more interesting than anything she did.

‘You look tired,’ she told him.

‘It’s the harvest,’ Ivo said. He rubbed the bridge of his nose.

That evening she walked down to the press-house. Sander was there, sweeping the floor; he did not hear her come in. The barn smelt of apples and wet stone.

At supper Ivo drank a full glass of the new cider, and nobody remarked on it.
