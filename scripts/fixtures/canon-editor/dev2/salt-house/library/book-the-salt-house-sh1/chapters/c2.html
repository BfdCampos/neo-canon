On the third Sunday it rained, and Uncle Tobiah did not take me to the dyke. He sat at the table instead and showed me how to tie a bowline with his good hand. He was my father’s brother, and he had my father’s way of not looking at you when he was pleased.

Granny Ruth unpinned her black hair at the window and brushed it out, a hundred strokes, the way she did every night.

“I have never once been out on that marsh in the dark,” Tobiah said, when she had gone up. “Not once in my life.”

I woke after midnight to the back door. From my window I saw Tobaiah coming in off the marsh with a lantern, wet to the knees, and Moss at his heels.

In the morning the gulls were walking on the slate roof again, and the whole house smelt of coal smoke, and nobody said a word about the night.
