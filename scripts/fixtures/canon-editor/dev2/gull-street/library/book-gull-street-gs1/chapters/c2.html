Mrs Peake’s front room was hot and full of china dogs. She sat very upright in a wing chair with a tartan rug on her knees.

“He’s a good boy, really,” she said. “His mother’s a saint. But that was his car, Inspector, and that was him.”

Agnes Peake, who looked eighty if she was a day, poured the tea with a steady hand.

Back at the station Gale read the file again. Danny’s cousin Kieran had a record for arson, two counts, both on Quay Road.

At six o’clock Danny Fell, all twenty-nine years of him, asked to speak to her. He was sitting with his head in his hands, and he said, without looking up, ‘I was there. I didn’t start it. I went to get Kieran out.’

Gale took the pencil from behind her ear and, for the first time anyone could remember, wrote something down.
