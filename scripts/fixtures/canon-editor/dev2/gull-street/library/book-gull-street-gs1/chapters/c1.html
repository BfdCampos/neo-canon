Detective Inspector Rhona Gale had worked the Harbour division for nine years and had never once been late for an interview. She was a square, sandy-haired woman of forty-four who wore the same navy raincoat in every weather and took her tea black. She kept a pencil behind her ear and never used it.

The interview room at Harbour Street station had no window. It had a table bolted to the floor, three plastic chairs and a radiator that clanked, and it smelt of instant coffee.

Danny Fell was already in it. He was twenty-six, by his file, a thin lad with a shaved head and a gold ring in one ear, and he worked nights at the bottling plant on Quay Road. His brother Kieran had been in the same room last spring.

“I was at my mum’s all night,” Danny said, before she had sat down. “Ask her. Ask anyone.”

"Nobody's asked you anything yet," said Gale.

“I don’t even own a car,” he said. “I’ve never been on Gull Street in my life.”

Gale opened the folder. The car outside 14 Gull Street on the night of the fire had been a silver Ford Focus, and it was registered to Daniel Fell of Mercer Row. A neighbour, Mrs Agnes Peake, had seen it there at ten. Mrs Peake was seventy-three and had lived on Gull Street all her life, and she told Gale that she never forgot a car.

“Mrs Peake says she saw you,” Gale said. “She says you waved.”

Danny laughed. He told her that the old woman was blind as a bat and that he was thirty-one, not twenty-six, and could prove it.

Gale let him talk. She had learnt long ago that men like Danny Fell told you the truth in the end, if only to correct you.
