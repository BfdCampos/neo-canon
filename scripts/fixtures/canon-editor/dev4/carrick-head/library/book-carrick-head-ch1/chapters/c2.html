I came on watch at 2300, as I did every night, and Ness was already asleep under the chart table.

The commander’s brown eyes went to the clock and back to me.

“You, Imogen, should have been in bed an hour ago,” she said.

Lieutenant Commander Okafor had run Carrick Head since the spring of 1941.

The commander’s Labrador, Bess, lifted her head when the door banged.

Dunn had been made Chief Petty Officer in March, and he wore the new badge on his sleeve.

I ran back down to Hut 21 with the signal, and the stove in there was smoking again.

Okafor was at breakfast in the wardroom when the answer came through.

Dunn took the long way round to the battery, by the cliff path.

Dunn was forty-four that winter, and Jo was twenty-six.
