Josiah Tolland Senior cast the great bell of St Ebba’s in the summer of 1889, and the town never let his family forget it.

The old man was sixty that year, a lean, stooping man with dark eyes and a white moustache.

His son, Josiah Tolland Junior, was nineteen and had his mother’s red hair. Everyone in the yard called the boy Young Joss.

Sergeant Ellis Pryce kept the police station on Bridge Street. He walked past the foundry every morning at eight.

The moulds were kept in Bay 9, a long brick shed by the river with a floor of sand.

The foundry kept a cat, a fat grey tom called Bellows, who slept on the warm bricks behind the furnace.

The boy did not look at his father when the bell was hung.

Pryce was forty, twenty years younger than the old man.
