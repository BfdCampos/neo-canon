The Lugger stood at the top of Quay Street in Polwenna, a narrow slate house with a blue door and four rooms to let.

Mrs Nora Hollis had kept it for thirty years. She was a stout, red-faced widow of sixty who rose at five every morning to bake.

Her dog was a wire-haired fox terrier called Pickle, a bitch with one black ear, who barked at every boat that came in.

Thomas Carew had the back room. He was twenty-four, a clerk at the harbour office, with ginger hair and brown eyes behind steel spectacles.

Carew’s sister Maud had the same freckles and their mother’s black hair.

A yellow oilskin hung behind the door of the back room.

Every evening at six Carew walked Pickle along the harbour wall.

Mrs Hollis had once had eleven lodgers at a time, before the war.
