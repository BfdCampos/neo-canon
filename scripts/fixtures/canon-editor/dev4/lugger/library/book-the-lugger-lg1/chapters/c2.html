Tom came down late, rubbing his dark hair dry with a towel.

Mrs Hollis’s little Jack Russell was waiting at the foot of the stairs with her lead in her mouth.

Mrs Hollis had gone to chapel, as she did every Sunday.

Carew straightened his tie and did not look at the clock.

He took the long way to the harbour office, past the lifeboat station.

Carew was at breakfast in the Ship by seven, because the Lugger’s range had gone out.

Carew was twenty-four and Maud was nineteen.

Pickle barked at the fishing boats all the way down Quay Street.
