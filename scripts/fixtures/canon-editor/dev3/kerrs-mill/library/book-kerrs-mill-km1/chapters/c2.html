By 1903 the Elder was dead and the Younger was the miller.

Visitors still came to see the mill race, which Absalom Kerr the Younger had built in the spring of 1871.

The Elder’s blue eyes looked down from the portrait over the stairs. The Younger’s own blue eyes were failing now, and Sal read the ledgers to him.

Kerr’s Mill stood on the west bank of the Hobb, as it always had, and its eleven windows still faced the water.

Sal kept the ledgers as his father had, and he rose every morning at five.

Smut was long gone. A grey cat with no name slept on the warm boards above the wheel.
