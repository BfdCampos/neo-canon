Hester Pell taught biology at Saint Aldhelm’s, in the old laboratory at the top of the science block.

She was thirty-one, and she had been form tutor to Year Eleven since September.

The science block stood next to the chapel, a red-brick building with green doors.

Assembly in the Great Hall was at ten to nine every morning, under the clock tower.

That morning Mr Wilde read out a letter from the bishop.

Dear girls, Saint Aldhelm’s has stood for two hundred years and has never once closed its doors. I pray it never will. Yours in faith, Bishop Crane of Wexcombe.

Jonah Wilde was sixty, bald, and walked with a cane. Mr Wilde had planted the cedar on the front lawn the year the school reopened after the war.

Hester stood at the window, furious, with her arms folded, while the girls filed out.

Saint Aldhelm’s had been founded in 1846 by the Fane sisters, and it had closed twice: once for the cholera and once for the war.
