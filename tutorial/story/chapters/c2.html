The fog came in at the second mile, the way it always did, as if it had been waiting for them to get far enough out. First the sea wall went, then the gulls, then the square tower of Marrowby church on its hill, until there was nothing left in the world but the causeway stones, the hiss of water in the channels and the two horses breathing.

Hesketh began to sing, quietly and badly, so that Wren would always know where he was. It was a song about a miller’s daughter, and it had a great many verses, and none of them ended well for the miller.

Wren’s grey eyes ached from staring into nothing. She counted the stones under Juniper’s hooves instead, the way her father had taught her: forty to the first withy, sixty to the Withy Post, and after that you stopped counting and started praying, or singing, which in Hesketh’s case came to much the same thing.

At the Withy Post the old road divides. The left fork runs to Marrowby on good stone. The right fork runs to nowhere in particular, and the Saltings keep what goes down it.

Wren felt it before she saw anything: a stiffening under the saddle, the ears pricked hard and forward, the weight shifting back onto the quarters. Juniper tossed his head and would not go on. Behind them, Ballast shook his mane and stood like a gatepost, perfectly content to wait for the end of the world.

“Water’s wrong,” said Hesketh.

Wren got down and walked forward with the lantern. Three strides on, where the stones should have been, there was only brown water sliding sideways, quick and quiet, carrying a tuft of sea-lavender along with it like a passenger. The channel had moved in the night. It did that, every spring, without asking anybody.

“That mare of yours is more mule than horse,” said Hesketh, “and thank God for it.”

They went round by the Long Bank, which added an hour and most of Wren’s patience. Juniper picked her way along it as delicately as a cat crossing a wet floor, and the fog went on being fog, and the sky above it turned the grey of old pewter, and then a little lighter.

At the second withy Hesketh turned back, as he had always meant to. “Mind the tide,” he said. “Mind the mare. Mind Mistress Fenn most of all.” Then he and Ballast were a shape, and then a sound, and then nothing at all.
