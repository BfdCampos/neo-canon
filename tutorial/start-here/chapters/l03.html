Canon can know your world in two ways. You can write its facts down yourself: lessons 4 to 9 all work like that, with the facts this tutorial wrote down for you. Or Canon’s reader can find them. It reads your story and picks out who and what is in it (people, places, creatures, things), what is true of each, and the key points of what happens. Without the reader, Canon only knows what you write by hand.

The reader runs entirely on this computer. It’s one download of a few gigabytes, and after that it works offline. Nothing you write is ever sent anywhere.

What to expect: ⌘' gives an instant Continuity Pass on what Canon already knows, with or without the reader. Reading a whole story is slower: from a few minutes to a quarter of an hour for this one on an everyday computer, and longer as a book grows. The reader pauses the moment you type and carries on after ten seconds of quiet, so you can keep writing all the while. On your own shelves Canon starts reading as soon as the reader arrives; here it waits for lesson 10, so the lessons in between find the world as they describe it.
