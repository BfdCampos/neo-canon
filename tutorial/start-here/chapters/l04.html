The story has three slips in it, planted on purpose. A writer makes them all the time: a mare who becomes “he” for one sentence, eyes that change colour, a town spelt two ways.

⌘' runs a Continuity Pass over the open book, the way ⌘; runs a spellcheck. Each slip gets a faint dotted underline in gold. The right-hand pane lists them; click one to go there.

Hover a mark to see why. The popover quotes the fact and its source, then offers a fix: his → her makes exactly that change, and ⌘Z puts it back. Not a slip tells Canon you meant it, and it won’t flag that line again unless you change it. ⌥Enter opens the slip under the caret without the mouse.

Press ⌘' again to clear every mark. Marks are never saved into your chapters.
