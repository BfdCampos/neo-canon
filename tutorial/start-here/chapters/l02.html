Canon keeps your world’s facts. Who is a mare. Whose eyes are grey. How the town is spelt. Each fact cites the passage it came from, word for word, so you can always see why Canon believes it.

When a later line disagrees with a fact, Canon says so quietly, after you stop typing. That’s all it does. It never writes a word of your story for you, and it runs on this computer: nothing you write is sent anywhere.

A Canon belongs to a shelf. Every book and notebook on the shelf is one world. This shelf, Tutorial, keeps a Canon already, so you can see it work before you set one up for yourself.

Canon’s words: an entry is one person, creature, place or thing. A fact is one thing true of it (Sex: mare). A source is the passage that says so. A slip is a line that seems to disagree.
